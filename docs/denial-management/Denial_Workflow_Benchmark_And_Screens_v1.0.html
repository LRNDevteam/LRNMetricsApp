Section 01

How we compare

Denial tooling splits into two jobs — working out why claims are denied, and getting them paid. We have both, on two stacks. The commercial market has largely converged on a third job we do not do at all: deciding which denials are worth the reviewer's next hour.

What the matrix says

We are ahead

The escalation loop nobody else has

Every vendor above models a denial as a job for the billing organisation. Ours is the only one that routes a claim out to the lab's own Client Manager or Account Manager, holds it in a Respond Escalation queue, accepts their answer and their documents, then returns it to the AR Manager.

That loop exists because we serve outsourced lab RCM, where the missing piece of information usually sits with the lab, not the biller. It is a genuine differentiator and it should be on the first slide of any competitive deck.

We are level

Queues, SLA, audit, line-level detail

Seven roles with distinct queue sets, server-side scoping from the JWT, an SLA clock, and a DenialTaskHistory row for every action are all at or above what the mid-market products describe.

CPT line-level denial detail with ICDComplianceStatus and DenialValidity puts us with the lab specialists — XIFIN and TELCOR — and ahead of the general hospital tools, which mostly stop at claim level.

We are behind

Nothing tells a reviewer what to work first

Our queues sort by status and date opened. Every competitor sorts by money it expects to recover. A reviewer with 340 open claims and six hours needs the second thing.

  • No overturn-probability score
  • No expected-recovery ranking
  • No appeal letter generation
  • No packet assembly or submission
  • Nothing at all before the denial happens
The three lines that matter. Overturn scoring, appeal generation and pre-submission prevention are the only rows where we are behind on capability rather than on polish. The first two are already scoped in the Agentic AI development plan — they are funding and sequencing questions, not design questions. The third is not scoped anywhere, and it is the one the lab specialists lead on: XIFIN markets predictive denial prevention as its headline claim, and prevention is the only intervention with no reviewer cost at all.

Recovery-ranked worklist

Compute overturn rate per payer × denial code × action category from closed claims. Add three columns and one sort option to My Worklist and Claim Assignment. Ship it before the agent work starts — it is independent of every gate in the agent plan and it is the change a reviewer will feel first.

Pre-bill denial prevention

Not in any current plan. Needs a decision before it can be estimated: do we get a hook into the billing stream before submission, or does prevention stay a report the lab acts on manually? The answer changes the size of this by an order of magnitude, and it is the one competitive gap that does not close itself as the agent work lands.

needs a decisionbilling-stream dependency

What we are asking for

  • Approve F1 as immediate work, ahead of and independent of the agent programme. It needs no model, no BAA and no new spend.
  • Agree that F5 ships with F2, not after it — the oversight screen is a control, not a reporting nicety.
  • Decide whether prevention (F4) is in scope at all. If it is, the billing-stream hook needs scoping now. If it is not, we should say so plainly, because it is the gap a competitor will lead with.
  • Commission a real evaluation of Waystar and XIFIN before the next planning cycle. This document is desk research; two vendor demos would tell us more than another matrix.
