Framework Overview
Every executive summary, billing rollup, or RCM report a lab owner reviews ultimately answers one of three questions. This document organizes all metrics, charts, and diagnostic checks around those three pillars, then provides a repeatable 7-step playbook for investigating any anomaly that shows up in any of them.
Pillar	Core question	Primary data source
1. Pipeline Health	Is volume flowing from sample collection through to a billed claim without getting stuck?	LIS export (specimen-level)
2. Revenue Realization	Of everything billed, how much converts to cash, and how fast?	Claim/billing export
3. Leakage & Risk	Where is money being lost — denials, write-offs, uncollected patient balances — and is it normal or a fixable problem?	Claim/billing export

The 7 Repeatable Diagnostic Checks
When any metric in any pillar moves in an unexpected direction, run through these checks in order — they are ordered roughly from most to least common root cause, so working through them in sequence resolves most anomalies quickly.
#	Check	Question it answers
1	Partial-period artifact	Is this a real trend, or did the report simply get cut off mid-month/mid-cycle?
2	Aging / backlog decomposition	Is a growing balance a healthy pipeline or a stuck backlog?
3	Ratio-driver breakdown	Is a moving ratio (denial %, write-off %) one bad slice (payer/panel/facility) or the whole book?
4	Reporting-artifact / wrong-date-field trap	Is this cohort really underperforming, or is it simply too young to have matured yet?
5	Cross-file reconciliation	Do two sections of the report — or two source files — that should tie out actually match?
6	Status-transition tracing	Did volume move from category A to category B (one event), rather than two independent problems?
7	Collections-reality check	Is a stated balance actually being collected, or is it just sitting on the books uncollected?

Diagnostic Check #1 (partial-period artifact) and Check #4 (wrong-date-field / maturity trap) apply across nearly the entire report simultaneously, not just to one metric.

Total Samples Volume Trend
Monthly collected sample volume has been stable in the 18,600–21,500 range over the past 8 months — no material volume decline. This rules out falling intake as a driver of any downstream revenue softness.


Total Samples & % Resulted
Volume itself is stable — no material change in intake. The story is in the % Resulted line: it holds at 98–100% through June, then drops to 85.7% in July. Per the framing above, this is Check #1: July’s samples simply have not all finished resulting yet, not a quality problem.
% Resulted = Resulted ÷ Total Samples × 100
How this helps: tracking this monthly, with the current partial month visually distinguished (e.g., a dashed or lighter bar), stops the lab owner from reacting to a false monthly dip and keeps attention on genuine multi-month drift instead.

Sample-Level Supplement: Full Pipeline Funnel & Backlog Age
The executive summary’s LIS Breakdown tracks Total Samples, Resulted, and Billed to Insurance as separate line items, but doesn’t show them as one connected funnel, and cannot age the backlog. Both gaps are filled here using the granular LIS export for this lab, covering the same period.
Sample-to-Claim Funnel

Funnel stage counts = COUNT(samples) at each stage: Collected → Resulted → Billed
Of all samples collected in the period, 98.3% get resulted — but only 75.2% ever become a billed claim. The 23.1-point gap between "resulted" and "billed" is the single largest volume leak found anywhere in this analysis, and it is invisible in the executive summary’s month-by-month view because that report never puts all three stages side by side in one chart.
Backlog Age — Resulted, Never Entered in PMS

Backlog Age = Extract Date – Collected Date, for samples Resulted but not yet entered in PMS, bucketed
This is the sample-level detail the Not Resulted trend above couldn’t provide Of the resulted-but-unbilled samples, the median age is 110 days, and 71% are over 60 days old. At this age, much of this volume is at meaningful risk of falling outside timely-filing windows and becoming permanently unbillable — a materially more urgent read than the raw "Not Resulted" count trend suggests on its own, since that line item only counts samples still awaiting a result, not samples that resulted but stalled afterward.
