Partially Paid $ — Monthly Trend

% Partially Paid $ = Partially Paid $ ÷ Total Billed $ × 100
Partially Paid $ is a small category in dollar terms (under $10K/month against a multi-million-dollar billed base) and stayed in a fairly stable $5K–$9.4K band through June before dropping to $495 in July — consistent with Check #1, since partial-payment determinations take time to land and July has not caught up yet. This line item is not worth independent escalation; it moves in step with the same maturity pattern as Fully Paid % and Collection Rate above.
Collection Rate — Insurance Payment $ ÷ Total Billed $
The underlying trend is genuinely positive Collection rate climbed steadily from 19.2% in January 2026 to 28.8% in May, essentially flat through June, before the July cutoff pulls it down to 15.2%. Read alongside the framing section: this is the dollar-denominated confirmation of the same "Fully Paid %" improvement seen in the PMS breakdown — a real, six-month positive trend that a same-month-only view would completely miss.

Patient Write-Off vs. Patient Balance — A Diverging Trend Worth Asking About
Worth confirming this is a policy change, not a stalled process Patient WO $ has fallen steadily and substantially — from $330K (Jul 2025) to $44.8K (Jul 2026) — while Patient Balance $ rose from roughly $23K to a peak of $135K (Jan 2026) and has held in the $100K–$135K range since. Fewer write-offs could mean better patient-collections discipline — or it could mean aged balances are simply accumulating on the books instead of being written off or actively collected. This is worth a direct question to the billing team: has the write-off policy or threshold changed, and is the patient-collections process (already flagged elsewhere in this project as largely non-functional at the claim level) keeping pace with this larger uncollected balance?

Fully Adjusted $ — Write-Off Reason Pareto

Reason-code % of Fully Adjusted = SUM(count or $ for reason code) ÷ SUM(Fully Adjusted) × 100
Applying Check #3 (ratio-driver breakdown) to Fully Adjusted: two reason codes — WOINS (insurance adjustment) and WOOON (out-of-network) — account for the large majority of 2026 write-off volume, together well ahead of every other reason combined. This report only provides counts by reason code, not $ amounts by reason; if the underlying PMS system can export $ by write-off reason, that would sharpen this from "which reason fires most often" to "which reason costs the most," which is the more decision-useful version of this chart.
