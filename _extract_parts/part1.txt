Not Resulted — Backlog Spike

Not Resulted trend = COUNT(Not Resulted) GROUP BY month
Not Resulted volume was negligible through 2025, began climbing in February 2026 (198, then 337, 335, 329), and then jumped sharply to 2,885 in July. Some of this July spike is Check #1 (samples collected late in the month haven’t had time to result), but the run-up starting in February predates that and is worth checking against the lab’s testing turnaround times. This summary alone can’t age these samples (Check #2 needs sample-level detail); the LIS export used earlier in this project is the right source for that follow-up.

Self Pay % vs. Client Bill % — A Real Categorization Shift
Real finding: a payer-mix shift starting May 2026 Self Pay dropped from a stable ~17–18% of total samples (Dec–Apr) to just 8.1% in May and 6.9% in July. Client Bill did the mirror opposite: from ~4–6% up to 16.0% in May, holding near 12% since. Diagnostic Check #6 (status-transition tracing) applies directly — this looks like volume moving from one billing category to another, not two independent problems, and the timing (May 2026) suggests a specific operational or client-contract change worth asking about directly.


Self Pay % = Self Pay ÷ Total Samples × 100Client Bill % = Client Bill ÷ Total Samples × 100

Billed Claims (PMS) — Cross-File Reconciliation
A persistent, structural gap — not a data error PMS Billed Claims consistently runs 900–1,800 claims higher than LIS "Billed to Insurance" every single month, with no exceptions in the last six months (e.g., July: 8,940 PMS vs. 8,059 LIS, a gap of 881). Applying Check #5 (cross-file reconciliation): a gap this consistent in direction and rough magnitude is almost certainly structural — PMS "Billed Claims" includes claims entered into AMD that are self-pay or client-bill (never routed to insurance), while LIS "Billed to Insurance" only counts the insurance-bound subset. Worth a one-time confirmation with the billing team that this is the correct explanation, since a real reconciliation break would look identical at a glance.


Reconciliation Gap = PMS Billed Claims – LIS Billed to Insurance
How this helps: tracking this gap monthly turns a one-time reconciliation exercise into an ongoing tripwire — if the gap suddenly widens or narrows beyond its normal ~900–1,800 band, that is the signal something changed in how claims are being routed or entered, worth catching before it compounds.
Fully Adjusted % of Billed Claims — Trend & Reason-Code Pareto

% Fully Adjusted = Fully Adjusted ÷ Billed Claims × 100
Fully Adjusted % has been falling steadily all year — 37.9% in February down to 11.5% in July — which at first glance looks like a good-news story (fewer claims being fully written off). Read this alongside Check #4: since July's claims are immature, many that will eventually be adjusted simply have not reached that determination yet, so some of this decline is a maturity artifact rather than a genuine improvement. The reason-code Pareto below shows what is driving the write-offs that have happened.

Reason-code % of Fully Adjusted = SUM(count for reason code) ÷ SUM(Fully Adjusted) × 100
Two reason codes — WOINS (insurance adjustment) and WOOON (out-of-network) — account for the large majority of 2026 write-off volume, well ahead of every other reason combined. This report only provides counts by reason code, not $ amounts by reason; if the PMS system can export $ by write-off reason, that sharpens this from "which reason fires most often" to "which reason costs the most."
Insurance Balance % of Billed Claims — The Headline PMS Finding
Real acceleration starting June — confirmed at claim level Insurance Balance held in a tight 4–6% band from mid-2025 through May 2026, then stepped up to 11.5% in June and 47.5% in July. The composition panel (right) shows exactly what is driving it: the "No Response" share of open claims has been climbing steadily since roughly March 2026 and now dominates the balance, while "Fully Denied" share has been shrinking as a proportion. This is the same signature already found and root-caused in the claim-level analysis — this executive-summary view confirms it independently and shows precisely when it started.


% Insurance Balance = Insurance Balance ÷ Billed Claims × 100No Response share = No Response ÷ (Fully Denied + No Response + Partially Denied) × 100
How this helps: the composition chart is what turns a scary headline number into an actionable one — a lab owner watching only the left-hand trend would (correctly) worry, but the right-hand chart tells them where to point that worry: it is overwhelmingly a payer-response backlog, not a denial-rate problem, which changes what the RCM team should chase first.
