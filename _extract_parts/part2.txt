The payment-date view resolves the Insurance Balance question from a different angle On a payment-date basis, Avg Paid per claim has stayed in a tight, unremarkable band all six months — $95–$106 in Toxicology, Validity Testing, and $100–$111 in Toxicology — including July. There is no July collapse once payment timing, not DOS cohort age, is the lens. This is independent confirmation, at the claim level, of the same conclusion the executive-summary framing section reached: the apparent July deterioration is a maturity artifact, not a change in how much payers actually pay once a claim is adjudicated.


Separately worth a question: Avg Allowed $ is gently declining on a DOS basis Avg Allowed per claim in Toxicology, Validity Testing has drifted down from $150.65 in February to $111.99 in July — a real, gradual decline unrelated to the payment-timing question above. This could reflect a shift toward lower-reimbursement payers or a change in test mix within the panel over time, and is worth a follow-up independent of the Insurance Balance conversation.

DOS-Cohort Maturity Curve
This chart is the direct evidence behind the "July is immature, not underperforming" conclusion used throughout this report. It plots cumulative % of allowed $ paid against days since date of service, one line per DOS-month cohort.

% of Allowed $ Paid (Day N) = Cumulative CarrierPayment through day N since DOS ÷ Cohort TotalAllowed × 100
June is tracking normally; July is simply too young to judge June's DOS cohort, at roughly 29 days old, is tracking in line with — even slightly ahead of — where May's and April's cohorts stood at the same age. That is direct evidence the underlying collection process has not broken. July has barely begun its lifecycle and cannot be judged against mature cohorts yet; a re-check once July reaches 45–60 days old would be the appropriate next look.
